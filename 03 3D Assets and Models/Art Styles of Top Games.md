# Art Styles of Top Games

A game-by-game read of art direction across the 27 chart games (see [[Top Games Chart 2026-11-02]]). Developer art breakdowns are rare for Roblox, so each row separates what a source states from what is an observation (marked `unverified`). The pattern: the biggest 2025-26 launches use deliberately simple, readable visuals; the 3D polish budget goes to the collectible (pet, brainrot, fish) and to a few signature scenes. For rules of thumb see [[Fidelity Tiers Low to High]] and [[When to Use Which Fidelity]].

## Sourced details

- **[[Grow a Garden]]:** Wikipedia says the aesthetic uses studded textures evocative of old-school Roblox. A 16-year-old created the first version in about three days, which confirms the early build leaned on Parts and built-in materials. Lesson: nostalgia style plus cheap rendering can still hit tens of billions of visits.
- **[[Steal a Brainrot]]:** Wikipedia describes the brainrots as voxel characters based on the Italian brainrot meme. Fan packs of the same memes exist as high-poly baked-texture rigged models (itch.io), which shows how far the in-game versions are simplified. Lesson: translate a meme into a chunky, readable voxel silhouette.
- **[[Adopt Me]]:** Uplift describes a hero set of the most-wanted pets defining the core style guide, bright colors, distinct silhouettes and family-friendly design; its 3D artist posting requires props, characters and environments that match the style and run on PC, mobile and console.
- **[[Pet Simulator 99]]:** Promotional coverage says each content drop brings a new visual theme (candy kingdoms to cosmic voids) and pets span rarity tiers from Common to Exclusive. Artist credits were not found.
- **[[Doors]]:** The hotel is called Victorian-era-esque by TheGamer; reviews note a subdued ominous palette unlike most bright Roblox games; a 2024 Retro Mode homage recreates old-school Roblox looks. The Mines floor added in August 2024 shows each floor getting a distinct material and lighting identity.
- **[[Dandys World]]:** Anthropomorphic "Toons" each have unique stats, and "Twisted Toons" are the corrupted versions; the design contrast is cute vs. distorted.
- **[[99 Nights in the Forest]]:** A survival horror set in a forest where players defend a campfire at night; developed by Alec Kieft, Cameron Angland and Matthew Hufton.
- **[[Dress To Impress]]:** Outfits are built from layered clothing in fashion subcultures; players equip up to 18 items. Eurogamer called its jankiness a "secret ingredient," confirming readability beats polish.
- **[[Rivals]]:** A German-language games.gg page describes clean, readable maps with clear sightlines; the game launched with 30 maps and 52 weapons per a stats site. Fans sell Rivals-styled futuristic lobbies, evidence of a recognizable look.
- **[[Blox Fruits]]:** Inspired by One Piece; world design is island-based.

## What the chart teaches

1. **Silhouette first:** every collectible reads at thumbnail size (see [[Characters Pets and Creatures]]).
2. **Cheap worlds, rich collectibles:** budget flows to the thing players trade and show off.
3. **Horror buys fidelity:** [[Doors]], [[Violence District]], [[Forsaken]] and [[99 Nights in the Forest]] use lighting more than polygons (see [[Lighting and Atmosphere]]).
4. **Nostalgia is a style:** studs, Retro modes and blocky avatars are brand assets.
