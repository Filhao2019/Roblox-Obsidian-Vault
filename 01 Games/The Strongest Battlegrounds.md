# The Strongest Battlegrounds

The Strongest Battlegrounds (TSB) is a free-for-all arena brawler by Yielding Arts, built on a One Punch Man fantasy: pick a character, fight in a small arena, build an ultimate meter, and unleash a short cinematic ultimate. It is widely credited with popularising the short-session "Battlegrounds" sub-genre on Roblox and won Best Fighting Experience at the 2024 Roblox Innovation Awards (per the game's own description). Study it for skill-based combat feel, readable move VFX and low monetization pressure. Compare [[Jujutsu Shenanigans]] (same genre, destruction-heavy).

## Core gameplay loop
- Minute to minute: spawn in a shared arena, punch/skill/dash/block, build the ultimate bar, kill others, die, respawn. Controls (from the game page): left-click punch, F block, Q dash (Q while ragdolled is a ragdoll cancel/evasive), double-tap W to run, G for ultimate mode, B for emote wheel.
- Each character has eight moves: four base plus four that are unlocked through awakening/ultimate mode (per RoWatcher). Skill is spacing, cooldown tracking and baiting blocks. See [[Combat Systems]].
- Session loop: join a server, chase streaks, then hop. Long term: mastering several characters, emotes and cosmetic passes; there is no heavy grind wall.

## Why it went viral
- A One Punch Man fantasy (Saitama-like "Bald Hero", Genos, Garou, Tatsumaki) with instantly legible power fantasy.
- Short, streamable rounds with "cinematic ultimate" moments that clip well for YouTube and TikTok.
- Free core roster; skill over spending.
- Creator-driven: original developer 22freshfrenchfries stepped back and collaborator Aspector took a larger role (per RoWatcher).
- Rival JJS spiked to 338K CCU in March 2026, reflecting how the Battlegrounds genre trades audience when one game ships a big update.

## Progression and retention
- No levels gate fights; retention comes from cosmetics, emotes (randomized emote spins with equal odds and no duplicates, per the Roblox description) and balance patches that change character viability.
- Update examples (May/July 2026 per RoWatcher): Monster Hero Hunter variant, a Tech Prodigy ultimate mode, ultimates/mobility overhauls. A leaked Cosmic Garou update reportedly adds a 3v3 mode and regional matchmaking (unverified).
- Community: 18.3 million group members and 1.6 million Discord users (RoWatcher).

## Monetization
- Seven game passes for cosmetics (custom capes, VIP chat tags) plus a 300 Robux Early Access pass for work-in-progress characters (RoWatcher). Gameplay power is not sold.
- Randomized rewards (emote/spin) are disclosed on the game page.

## Art direction and 3D assets
- Low-poly Roblox avatars on small open arenas with destructible-looking craters (crater decals/terrain deformation appear in clips, unverified), mostly flat desaturated skyboxes so VFX stand out. Character identity is carried by hair, outfit and accessory (cape, cyborg limbs) on a standard R15 rig. See [[Art Styles of Top Games]].

## UI and GUI
- Minimal HUD: health bar, a row of four move slots with cooldown fill and key hints, an ultimate bar, and a kill/streak feed. Emote wheel opens with B. Cooldown overlays are the main UI feedback (unverified exact layout). See [[UI Breakdown of Top Games]].

## Animation
- Heavily animated per-move sequences: punch combos, dash with afterimages, ragdoll states with cancel input, and ultimates with pre-rendered-feel camera cuts. The ultimate temporarily takes camera control with a close-up and dramatic zoom (unverified details). See [[Combat and Ability Animations]] and [[Animation MOC]].

## SFX and music
- Heavy impact "thud" hits, whoosh on dashes, voice-style shout text for ultimates, and a loud musical sting during ultimate mode (unverified). See [[Combat and Impact Sounds]].

## VFX
- Shockwave rings and dust on hits, speed lines on dashes, screen-wide flashes and large energy beams on ultimates (the Serious Series style). Effects are short and high-contrast so an opponent can read the move before it lands. See [[VFX of Top Games]] and [[Combat and Ability VFX]].

## Systems and code worth studying
- Hit detection with client prediction and server validation, stun/ragdoll state machines, per-move cooldowns, block/parry windows, awakening meter, and server-side damage. See [[Combat Systems]], [[Networking and Remotes]] and [[Security and Anti-Exploit]] (known issues include exploit scripts and teamers).

## Lessons to steal (patterns, not content)
- Eight moves per character (4 base + 4 unlock) is enough depth for competitive play and few enough to learn quickly.
- Give a dedicated defensive escape (ragdoll cancel) so stuns never feel hopeless.
- Make the ultimate a spectacle with a camera moment: it creates shareable clips and a comeback mechanic.
- Keep core roster free and sell cosmetics, which holds like ratio steadier than pay-to-win.
- Ship frequent balance patches; the meta shuffle is itself retention.
- Plan anti-teaming and anti-exploit from day one in free-for-all lobbies.
